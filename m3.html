History from MacTutor (St Andrews), “The missing planet”: Piazzi first saw Ceres on 1 January 1801 and followed it for about six weeks, a geocentric arc of only about three degrees, before it was lost near the Sun. Gauss, then 24, computed its orbit from three observations, and von Zach found Ceres again on 7 December 1801, very close to Gauss’s position. Gauss went on to develop least squares from this work. The orbit drawn here is simplified to a flat ellipse (Ceres: a ≈ 2.77 AU, e ≈ 0.08, period about 4.6 years), so dates and angles are approximate.
