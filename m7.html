History from the Apollo Lunar Surface Journal and F. Fjeld, “The biggest myth about the first Moon landing” (Horizons, 2013): Charlie Duke called “Sixty seconds” and “Thirty seconds” before “Contact light”; propellant slosh had turned the low-level light on early, so the real margin was larger than the calls suggested. The descent engine gave up to about 10,000 lbf (45 kN), and hovering took about a quarter of that. Masses, propellant and the guidance law here are representative, not flight data: 7,300 kg at 150 m, 380 kg of usable propellant, exhaust speed 3,050 m/s, lunar gravity 1.62 m/s².
